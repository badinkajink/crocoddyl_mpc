A crocoddyl MVP for the braced lean

Session 12, third pass. The enumeration already produces exactly what a DDP wants — a contact schedule and a certified pose — so this wires that into crocoddyl and asks it to produce the trajectory that gets there. It solves. A 2 s plan from stand puts the reaching hand 1.1 mm from the target, inside torque limits, and lands 0.69 rad from the certified pose where the hand-authored keyframe ramp lands ~1.5 rad away. It does not yet survive MuJoCo: the plan drives the bracing gripper 68 mm through the tabletop, and neither replay mode establishes brace contact.

Read this first. The headline is a solver result, not a robot result. crocoddyl solved the optimal control problem it was given; the MuJoCo replay of that solution fails. Sections 5 and 6 are the honest part of this page and they are where the remaining work is. Nothing here has been near hardware.

1 · Why this is the right shape for the problem

The previous page established what the current stack actually does: agent_planner=5 is Cross Entropy with sampling_trajectories=10, n_elite=6, sampling_spline_points=3 and agent_horizon=1.0 — a 10-sample search over 81 dimensions looking 1 second ahead at a 14 second maneuver. The hand-authored keyframe ramp is the plan; CEM is a short-horizon stabilizer tracking a setpoint being dragged past it.

The complaint I made against DDP earlier — that a prescribed contact schedule is a liability — was backwards. The enumeration already produces a prescribed schedule, exhaustively, with static feasibility certified per mode. That is precisely crocoddyl's required input. So the division is: enumeration chooses where to brace, crocoddyl produces how to get there, and MJPC/a WBC tracks the result.

2 · The model bridge, and 0.78 kg that was not there

Every number this study has produced came out of the MJCF; crocoddyl plans on a Pinocchio model built from h1_2_magpie.urdf. Two models of one robot from one source, and the plan is worthless if they disagree — so the bridge ships with a parity test rather than inline index arithmetic. Three things had to be reconciled: the URDF's 12 gripper linkage joints (frozen, since the MJCF fuses each gripper to one rigid body), the MJCF's free-floating table (nq=41 is robot + table; the robot is qpos[0:34]), and the free-joint quaternion order (MuJoCo w,x,y,z vs Pinocchio x,y,z,w).

Kinematics agreed immediately. Mass did not. The URDF is missing back_equipment — 0.670 kg bolted to torso_link in the MJCF, with no URDF link at all — plus 0.056 kg per gripper. That is 0.78 kg, and it is on the torso, which pitches ~32° in the brace and therefore carries the longest moment arm of any payload on the robot. Left alone, every contact force crocoddyl predicted would have been quietly low against the numbers the static QP certified. The bridge now overwrites all Pinocchio link inertias with MuJoCo's composite inertias, so the dynamics match by construction.

3 · The enumeration does not discriminate at this target

All 32 of 32 contact subsets over {elbow, forearm, palm, hip, torso} are statically admissible at the session-12 target. Bracing is not required here — legs-only already balances at max |τ|/limit = 0.46 — it buys effort:

Two consequences. First, this target is a weak discriminator for contact selection — if the point is to show bracing matters, the target needs to be further out, where legs-only stops being admissible. Second, the modes that win are the ones that recruit the torso, which is consistent with the S11 finding that torso is the joint with headroom. The MVP below deliberately uses elbow+forearm instead of the ranked winner, because that is the mode the existing MJPC strategy and the S11 stability result both use, so the comparison is like-for-like.

4 · The optimal control problem, and the five things that made it solve

Two phases over a prescribed schedule: an approach (feet only) that lowers the CoM and swings the bracing arm over the table, then a braced phase (feet + brace) that settles onto q*, reaches the target and comes to rest. Feet are 6D contacts at the sole frames with wrench cones; brace sites are 3D contacts with friction cones; torque limits are hard box bounds on SolverBoxFDDP.

Getting from "written" to "solves" took six distinct blockers, five of them real modelling errors rather than tuning. Recording them because every one cost more than it should have:

SymptomActual causeFix
SIGSEGV / MemoryError / basic_string::_M_create, varying between identical runs The pip crocoddyl cmeel wheel. Contact-cone residuals identify their contact by dynamic_cast; the wheel defines those symbols in two libraries and the typeinfo in only one, so the cast returns garbage and the data constructor memmoves a bogus size. A conda-forge env (conda activate croco). This is why every croco script must be run with that interpreter.
MemoryError building the problem FrictionCone handed to a 6D foot contact. It is the 3D object; the 6D one is WrenchCone. No type error — a mis-sized allocation. Wrench cones on the feet, friction cones on the brace. Better anyway: the wrench cone's extra rows are the centre-of-pressure staying inside the 0.20 × 0.08 m sole.
Segfault assembling the ShootingProblem The joint-limit cost used 0 * x_star as its reference, which zeroes the floating base quaternion; every state.diff then normalizes by zero. state.zero().
Solver stalls: 17 iterations, no step accepted, regularization walks 1e−8 → 1e9 Warm start interpolated the 27 joint angles while holding the base fixed, swinging the legs under a pelvis that never descends — both feet end 0.39 m off the floor, violating the contacts the problem is built on. pin.interpolate over the whole configuration.
Braced phase will not converge from any cone weight Feet + brace is 18 constraint rows on 33 DOF and the contacts are redundant: the KKT system is singular, so the force the cone cost differentiates is arbitrary. inv_damping = 1e-4. Measured: damping 0 or 1e−6 fails at every setting tried; 1e−4 converges at all of them. This is the same regularizer the static QP already needs — its w_lam min-norm term.
Cones still fail on the full problem The interpolated guess has zero velocity, so its implied forces are nothing like the real ones and the cone barriers evaluate at ~3150 against ~2.8 for everything else. Two-stage continuation: solve with cones off, then re-solve with cones warm-started from that. Stage 1 is not a throwaway — it is what makes stage 2 well-posed.

Two further notes. The impulse node at touchdown is implemented but off by default: with nu=0 it makes SolverBoxFDDP return after a few iterations with an uninitialised stop value. Physically it costs little here — a hand placed deliberately on a table lands at near-zero velocity. And the terminal weight must stay modest (1e0 converges; 1e1 and above return to the stalled signature), so q* enters as a bias, not a hard terminal constraint.

6 · Where it fails: MuJoCo

The plan does not survive contact with the simulator, in two independent ways.

6.1 The bracing gripper goes through the table

Driving the planned configurations into MuJoCo and reading the narrowphase: the deepest robot–table penetration over the plan is −67.8 mm, and it is the left_magpie_gripper — the gripper on the bracing arm, which is not in the elbow+forearm contact subset and was therefore unconstrained. The certified pose q* has no such penetration.

Extending the half-space barrier from the selected sites to every arm site improved this from −84 mm to −67.8 mm and no further, and the reason it cannot go further is worth stating: at q* itself the gripper's lowest collision corner sits 7.4 mm below the tabletop plane, hanging over the table edge where there is no table to hit. A z-barrier cannot express "below the top but outside the footprint", so tightening it enough to exclude the gripper would forbid the certified pose. This needs real collision geometry (ResidualModelPairCollision against a table box), not a smarter half-space. Reweighting the bracing arm 10× toward q* was tried as a shortcut and destroyed the solve outright — back to the stalled 17-iteration signature.

6.2 Neither replay establishes the brace

The actuators are position servos (kp=150, kd=5, read off the model), so a faithful replay inverts the servo law at the planned state, ctrl = qplan + (τplan + kd·vplan)/kp; the plan runs at dt = 0.02 against MuJoCo's 0.002, so each node is held for 10 substeps. Both are done. Both replays still fail:

A 0.9 rad open-loop tracking error over 2 s is not a small discrepancy to be tuned away. The plan is an open-loop torque trajectory computed against rigid prescribed contacts; MuJoCo has compliant contacts, and the brace contact that the plan is leaning on never forms — so the support the plan assumes is simply absent, and the robot goes wherever the residual torques take it. This is exactly the gap a tracking controller exists to close, and it says the missing piece is the third stage of the chain: enumeration → crocoddyl → WBC/MJPC tracking. Feeding the plan open-loop was never going to work, and the numbers above are what "never going to work" looks like.

7 · Can crocoddyl do the contact selection too?

Asked, and the answer is no, not natively — crocoddyl optimizes over a given contact schedule. There is no complementarity or integer machinery in it, so contact-implicit trajectory optimization is out of scope for the library as it stands.

What is available, and cheap, is enumerate-and-rank at the trajectory level: the mode set is only 32 wide, a solve is 2.4 s, so re-ranking all admissible modes by dynamic cost rather than static effort is about two minutes of compute. That is a strictly better selector than the static QP, because it prices the cost of getting into the mode, which the static QP cannot see by construction. The harness supports it (--mode takes any admissible subset); it has not been run, because the single-mode result does not yet survive replay and ranking modes on a broken replay would be measuring noise.

8 · State, and what I would do next

Working and verified: the Pinocchio/MuJoCo bridge with its parity test; the mode enumeration with per-mode poses saved; the two-phase OCP with staged continuation, which converges reliably; the MuJoCo replay harness with correct servo inversion and substepping; video rendering.
Not working: the plan penetrates the table with the bracing gripper (−68 mm); no replay establishes brace contact; open-loop tracking error 0.9 rad. Untested: every mode except elbow+forearm; every target except the session-12 one; the impulse-node path.

In priority order:

  1. Collision geometry for the table (ResidualModelPairCollision + a Pinocchio GeometryModel carrying the table box). This is the blocking defect and the half-space cannot substitute for it.
  2. Close the loop on the replay. The plan is a reference trajectory, not a controller. Either hand it to MJPC as the setpoint sequence (replacing the hand-authored keyframe ramp — which is the actual proposal) or track it with a WBC. Comparing open-loop DDP against closed-loop MJPC, as section 6.2 does, flatters MJPC unfairly.
  3. Move the target outward until legs-only stops being admissible, so contact selection has something to select and the reach result means something.
  4. Then, and only then, dynamic re-ranking of the 32 modes.
