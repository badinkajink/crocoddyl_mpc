The reach envelope was censored, and MJPC does not brace

Session 11. The headline number from the last page — “bracing buys +0.20 m of reach” — was measuring arm length, not balance. Fixing it shows what bracing actually buys: not more reach, but reach that survives a margin requirement. Separately: the torso e-stop does not trace to the safety layer, friction stops mattering below μ = 0.3, and MJPC sampling never established a brace in 12 s.

1. The math

Rendered properly this time. Three stages: a pose, a force distribution at that pose, and a region of poses. Everything downstream — slip, margin, push, the reach envelope — is one of these three with a different objective.

1.1 Pose: the IK step

Gauss–Newton over the 33 robot DOFs, one quadratic program per iteration. The step δq solves

minδq 12 δq⊤Gδq −a⊤δq s.t. nc⊤Jcδq ≥dc−ε ∀c∈C −Δ≤δqi≤Δ

with G=∑kwk2Jk⊤Jk+λI, a=∑kwk2Jk⊤ek over the task terms k (reach, brace sites, pinned feet, posture), C the near-contacts inside the look-ahead margin, dc their signed distances, and Δ=0.05 the trust region. Non-penetration is an inequality inside the QP, not a penalty — that is what stopped the solver walking through the table.

1.2 Pose: aligning the gripper’s flat face (new)

To brace on the side of the gripper the wrist has to roll until that face meets the wood. Writing a for the face normal (a body-fixed axis, in world) and t=(0,0,−1), the residual and its Jacobian are

r=a×t , ∂r∂ω = [at⊤ −(a·t)I]

from a˙=ω×a. Using the scalar cost 1−a·t instead has zero gradient at both the aligned and the anti-aligned pose, so the solver can park the face pointing at the ceiling; the cross-product residual has no such stationary point on the wrong side.

1.3 Force: static equilibrium at a fixed pose

With q˙=q¨=0,

S⊤τ +∑i Ji⊤λi =g(q)

splitting the 6 unactuated floating-base rows from the 27 actuated ones:

∑i Ji,1:6⊤ λi =g1:6 τ=g7:33 −∑i Ji,7:33⊤ λi

g(q) is qfrc_bias at rest (pure gravity), not mj_inverse — the latter folds in qfrc_constraint, i.e. whatever the pose happens to be penetrating, which at the seed keyframe is 20 kN against the 668 N actually being balanced.

1.4 Force: the friction pyramid

Per contact, in the contact frame — λn along MuJoCo’s own resolved normal, not world z:

λn≥0 , |λt,k| ≤μλn2 ,k=1,2

Two rows per tangent, not one negated row. Negating the whole row pins |λt| to the boundary instead of bounding it, and every LP comes back infeasible — that bug cost a session (S10c.1).

1.5 Region: which centres of mass are holdable

The multi-contact generalisation of the support polygon (Bretl & Lall 2008; Caron et al. 2015). For CoM c, robot mass M, and an optional horizontal disturbance f applied at the CoM:

∑i Ri⊤λi = (0,0,Mg) −f ∑i pi× Ri⊤λi = (−Mgcy ,Mgcx ,0) −c×f

The moment is independent of cz, so this is genuinely a 2-D set and needs no height assumption. Solved twice: contact-only (these six rows plus the cones — exact) and actuated (also |τ|≤τmax, which freezes g(q) at the solved pose and is therefore a linearisation, reported separately for that reason). The boundary is ray-shot at 10° resolution with 18 bisection steps; margin is the distance from the pose’s own CoM to that boundary.

1.6 The derived scalars

ui= ‖λt,i‖ μλn,i ρ(μ)= maxj |τj| τjmax

slip utilisation (u=1 is on the cone) and peak normalised actuator effort. The friction threshold this page reports is

μτ*= min{μ: |ρ(μ)− ρ(∞)|≤δ}

with ρ(∞) computed directly, by deleting the cone rows and leaving the normals unilateral — not extrapolated from the tail of a sweep.

2. Friction: solved for, not assumed

Your point stands, so MU stopped being a constant. Since the IK never sees μ (it is purely kinematic), one pose per contact set is re-scored at every μ — same pose, same contacts, same Jacobians, one parameter moving.

The answer you wanted: μτ*≤0.3. Above μ=0.3 the pose’s peak torque is within 1 % of its frictionless limit and does not move again out to μ=5. So at the study’s μ=0.6 the torque numbers are characterising the joint envelope, with a 2× cushion. Handbook dry values put steel-on-wood around 0.3–0.5 and rubber-on-wood at 0.7–1.0, so this holds for any plausible contact material — though that is a literature range standing in for a measurement, not a measurement. Every torque and reach result below is friction-independent in that sense.
But this does not hold at the envelope edge, which is where it matters. Re-running the same sweep at x=1.35 m — the elbow+forearm strength limit rather than an interior pose — gives μτ*=0.8 for elbow+forearm and 1.5 for elbow+forearm+palm. At the study's μ=0.6 the limiting pose is therefore still friction-affected, and the reach envelope in §3 would move with a grippier table. The interior result above does not transfer to the boundary, because the boundary is exactly where shear demand is highest. So the previous page's “the limit is friction, not torque” was closer to correct than I first credited, and the §3 envelope numbers are conditional on μ=0.6.
And the region metric never becomes friction-free at all. μregion* is never reached — at μ=5 the actuated equilibrium region is still 6–10 cm short of its frictionless limit. The region’s boundary is exactly where shear demand peaks, so extra friction keeps buying region long after it has stopped changing what the pose costs. Concretely: the margin, max-push and reach-envelope columns everywhere on this page remain functions of the assumed μ=0.6 and would grow with a grippier table. Only the interior peak-torque figures are friction-free. Measuring μ on the real tabletop is still the single cheapest experiment on the list, and it has not been de-risked.

4. Contact-site rework

4.1 The palm is now the side of the gripper

The old “palm” was a point on the wrist-yaw link axis, 130 mm out — the wrist housing, driven into the table by wrist pitch. It is now the centre of the flat side face of *_gripper_collision on the *_magpie_gripper body, and the wrist rolls to put that face down (§1.2). Measured on the model:

Rolling swings the yaw axis from mostly-vertical (yaw swings the hand sideways) to mostly-lateral (yaw pitches the hand into the table), and yaw has 2.75× the travel for the same torque ceiling. The solver finds roll 85° unprompted at elbow+forearm+palm, with alignment residual 0.001.

This recovers the contact you said was being lost. With the old site, elbow+forearm+palm could not place the palm. It now places all three. And the wrist yaw is finally load-bearing and therefore characterisable: at a palm-only brace it carries 4.12 / 8.55 N·m = 48 % of its clamped budget, the second-most-loaded joint in the robot. That number did not exist before, because the force was being applied proximal of the joint.

It also exposes why the palm is the weak contact. At μ=0.6 in the triple brace the palm runs at slip utilisation 0.996 — exactly on the cone — carrying 10 N normal and 6 N tangential. It is not adding much because friction will not let it, which is the concrete version of your friction point.

From 50 mm to 110 mm along the forearm link — the distal end of left_forearm_pad, the capsule that is the forearm’s actual brace surface (fromto 0.02 → 0.11). Past that is the wrist-roll joint at 0.121, so this is as far as the modelled pad goes. Site separation at the seed:

Scored over the entire rollout, not the final frame — strategy 22 is an eight-phase brace-and-return sequence (stand_up → forearm_brace_lean → forearm_brace_reach → forearm_brace_release → standback ×3 → stand_up), so its last frame is the robot deliberately standing back up. What this is and is not evidence for. It is one configuration: one strategy, one start pose, one horizon, the shipped cost weights. A negative result here does not prove sampling cannot do it — it shows that the task as currently configured does not get there, and that the gap is not a torque gap. It is also consistent with your read that this is what a phase-structured optimiser like crocoddyl is built for: the brace is a discrete contact-mode decision, and a sampler perturbing around a nominal that never touches the table gets no gradient toward touching it. The 30 s runs already rule out the cheapest alternative explanation.

Your reformulation is the right one and it is now the primary experiment. Hip pitch is commanded (both hips, as a heavily weighted posture target — hard-clamping it removes a DOF the pinned-foot task needs and the feet drift 40 mm), the reach target is set deliberately unreachable so the arm extends as far as that flexion allows, and reach is measured.

This confirms the mechanism and corrects its conclusion. At zero margin every set reaches about the same distance — 1.35–1.40 m — so bracing does not buy reach outright. What it buys is reach that survives a margin requirement: legs-only decays from 1.386 → 1.232 m by m ≥ 0.05 and cannot hold m ≥ 0.10 at all, while the braced sets sit flat at 1.384 m across every threshold. The right way to state the benefit is therefore “the same reach with 0.10 m of margin instead of none”, not “30 cm further”.

One refinement to the causal chain. Reach is not monotone in hip flexion: it peaks at hip ≈ −0.90 rad (1.384 m) and falls to 1.204 m by −1.80 rad. Past the optimum, deeper flexion lowers the shoulder, and holding the hand at a fixed target height then costs forward extension. So “more stability → more hip actuation → more reach” holds only up to that optimum; beyond it the extra flexion is spent fighting the target’s height. Hip-pitch torque meanwhile climbs 0.28 → 0.49 of its clamped limit and the brace normal load falls 126 → 62 N, i.e. the deep-flexion poses shift load off the brace and back onto the legs — the opposite of what the brace is for.

11.4 MJPC does not fail to find the brace — it rejects it

Agreed on the conclusion, but the evidence turned out to say something sharper than “sampling cannot find it”, and it changes the recommendation. testspeed gained --start_qpos, so the planner can be started on top of the QP answer. Handed the braced pose directly — in contact, margin +0.233 m:

Within half a second it stands back up. It leaves a pose with +0.233 m of margin for one with −0.05, drives the hand away from the target (0.03 → 0.56 m), drops from four contacts to one, and saturates its actuators at peak τ = 1.111 doing it. Two repeats, same behaviour. This is not a search failure — the planner was placed at the optimum and walked away from it.
Which means a solver swap is not yet a testable proposition. If the cost function’s optimum is the upright pose, then crocoddyl, iLQG, or a MuJoCo-native DDP will all converge to the upright pose — faster, and just as wrong. The current experiment does not distinguish “sampling is the wrong algorithm” from “the task is asking for something else”, and the second explanation now has direct evidence behind it.

It is also worth being clear about what crocoddyl would and would not give us: DDP-family solvers take a prescribed contact schedule and produce a dynamically feasible trajectory that respects it. They do not search over contact modes either. We already have the schedule — that is exactly what this study’s QP produces. So the missing piece is not mode search; it is a trajectory between two poses under a known contact sequence.

Recommendation: do not spin out crocoddyl yet. The cheap experiment that has to come first is making the QP pose the tracked reference and re-running — if MJPC holds a pose it is actually asked to hold, the handoff can stay where it is and the architecture becomes “QP solves the contact set and pose offline, MPC tracks and refines online”, which is what the original plan sketched. If it still will not hold it, that is the result that justifies the pinocchio dependency, and it would come with a concrete failure mode to hand to the next solver rather than an average cost. The homespun MuJoCo-native route only becomes attractive after that second measurement, because until then we do not know whether we are buying a better solver or a different bug.

9. What this leaves open

  • Which safety config the robot launches with. If it is tight, the torso e-stop is 20 N·m against the node’s 36 — a plan inside the analysis budget trips the layer. Cheapest check on this list, and the only one with a hardware consequence.
  • μ on the actual tabletop. Still unmeasured, now with a bounded blast radius: it moves margin and max-push, not torque or reach.
  • Make the QP pose the tracked reference and re-run MJPC. This is now the top of the list: it is the experiment that decides whether the handoff needs a new solver at all (§11.4).
  • The Δy = −0.10 stance is still an offline choice. Nothing has checked that the robot can get to it without stepping, which is the constraint the whole keyframe campaign is built around.
  • elbow+forearm+hip is still censored — its envelope ends on reach at 1.25 m. Its true strength limit is unmeasured, and it is the set with the largest margin.
  • The MJPC result needs a second configuration before it can carry weight: strategy 34 (the 4-phase mission), a longer horizon, and cost weights that actually reward the contact.
