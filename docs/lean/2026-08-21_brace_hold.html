The brace was never holding

A braced lean that creeps in every contact mode, a contact gain that was zero for the study's whole life, and a load path that no mode ever named.

2026-08-21 · S22 · measured on studies/runs/mycell, in-process MuJoCo, closed loop, 40 s holds

Three complaints started this session, all from watching the twin rather than reading a number: the brace drifts off the table on a hold; the planner is stuck on elbow+palm with no way to try anything else; and the arm is clearly collapsing even when the run reports success. All three turned out to be the same story told at different levels, and the bottom of it is a single constant that has been 0.0 since the first crocoddyl plan in this study.

What changed. Default contact mode elbow+palm → elbow+forearm. Default Baumgarte position gain 0 → 50. Contact mode is now a live dropdown that switches without resetting the robot. A wrist site exists, because that is where the load has been going all along. On a 40 s hold the pelvis sink went from 49.7 mm and still falling to 18.0 mm and settled, and brace drift from 52.4 mm to 3.6 mm.

1. Kp = 0 means the brace has no reason to stay put

crocoddyl's contact models are constraints, not costs. Each one fixes the acceleration of a contact point through a Baumgarte-stabilised relation:

contact stabilisation

ac+ Kd⁢vc+ Kp⁢perr =0

with perr the offset between where the site is and the spot the static QP certified for it.

The study has always run CONTACT_GAINS = [0.0, 50.0]. With Kp=0 that relation says this point does not accelerate, and its velocity decays — it says nothing at all about where the point is. Position error has no restoring term. So every millimetre the brace loses, to the servo, to the plant/model gap, to the seam of a chained behaviour, is kept.

This was documented and still surprising. croco_plan's own docstring says “once the phase starts the site stays wherever it arrived.” That reads as a statement about the plan. It is also a statement about the hold, and the hold is where this study spends its time.

The ladder is not monotonic, and that matters more than the winner. Kp = 10 is much worse than Kp = 0 — 81 mm of sink against 49.7 — and Kp = 20 and Kp = 30 straddle zero tail rate in opposite directions. Do not read this table as “more gain is better” and interpolate: a weak restoring term appears to fight the warm start (solved at the plan's own gain) without being strong enough to win, and Kp = 100 buys the drift back while pushing peak torque to 0.90 of limit.

The drift traces are the cleaner picture. At Kp = 0 the brace slides away and keeps sliding. From Kp = 30 up it walks out a few millimetres and then stops, which is what a hold is supposed to look like and what no configuration of this study had produced before.

2. The declared contact set does not allocate load

The second complaint — that elbow+forearm produces no forearm force — is correct, and it generalises further than it looks.

A contact in this formulation is a prescribed kinematic constraint. It fixes where a site may go; the force falls out of the dynamics afterwards. So a mode's name lists the sites that are constrained, not the links that carry weight, and the two routinely disagree. Here is every brace-arm contact at the certified elbow+forearm pose:

left_elbow_linkleft_forearm_pad0.00 N−1.44 mmyes — the forearm site lives here

The forearm pad is 1.44 mm inside the wood and carrying nothing. The arm is a beam supported at the elbow and at the wrist, and the middle of it bridges. Over the 40 s hold the split is elbow 112 N, forearm 2.7 N, and 32 N through an uncommanded wrist contact.

This is the same pathology already known for elbow+palm, whose palm carries 0.00 N for an entire hold, but with a different cause. The palm is 22 mm in the air — the certification says so itself now:

[contact_select] WARNING: no narrowphase contact on 'palm' (left_magpie_gripper);
the QP will balance a force at the site instead, 22.3 mm above the tabletop.
A certification that passes here is a certification about a point in the air.

The forearm, by contrast, is genuinely touching and genuinely unloaded.

The geom that looks disabled is the one doing the work. left_wrist_pad has contype = 0 and conaffinity = 0 — and collides anyway, through an explicit <contact><pair> (the model carries 20 of them; the pad reports its gap at exactly −2.000 mm, a declared pair margin). Meanwhile every gripper geom has contype = 1, conaffinity = 1 and is simply 9–33 mm above the table. Nothing was ever switched off; show_gripper moves geom_group and has never touched the collision bits.

So the wrist became a site

It is the load path in every mode, and until now no mode could name it. The site sits at the centre of the pad's local −z face — measured from where the contact points actually land (z = −0.0415 against a 0.0425 half-extent), not guessed from the box.

Additive on purpose. SITES5 and ARM_SITES are unchanged. Every certified cell and every ranking in this study was computed over that 5-tuple, and quietly widening it would rewrite what existing records mean. The wrist enters only when asked for: croco_modes --sites elbow,forearm,palm,wrist,hip,torso. Re-certified at this cell's target that is 25 admissible modes of 64, with elbow+wrist ranked 2nd by static effort.

elbow+forearm holds best and keeps the widest support margin, and is the default. elbow+wrist is the interesting runner-up: it ties on tail rate, has the least drift of anything measured (−0.3 mm — it comes back), and does it at 0.59 peak torque against 0.86, which is a large amount of actuator headroom to have in hand on hardware. Its cost is a narrower support margin. Worth a look before this goes on a real robot.

The static ranking does not predict the dynamic one. croco_sweep's effort ranking put elbow+palm first — a ranking computed with a palm force that does not exist in it. The dynamic hold puts it fourth. Any mode selection made from the QP's effort number alone should be re-checked on a hold.

4. Switching contact mode without letting go

The reason nobody had run that comparison is that the panel folded two independent questions into one dropdown. Task is what the robot is doing — approach and brace, stand, come back off. Mode is which links are on the table. Only the product of the two names a plan file, so a single list of tasks made “the same brace, in a different mode” unsayable, and comparing modes meant editing a command line and restarting.

They are separate dropdowns now, and the cell decides what is on offer: discover_plans reads every plan_*.json and classifies it from the plan's own fields — n_return > 0 is a recovery, an empty subset is a stand, anything else is a brace — never from the filename. Two plans claiming the same slot are reported, not silently resolved by alphabetical order, which is a real state that occurred while building this.

Switching mode chains: the episode ends, the plant is not reset, and the new plan is joined at the node nearest the measured pose. Measured on a live elbow+forearm → elbow+palm switch while braced:

What it cannot do is put a limb on the table that is not already there. Switching into a mode whose contact set is not satisfiable from the current pose is a real seam, and the chain_* fields in the run artifact are what say whether it was.

The Baumgarte gain rides the same machinery. It cannot be a slider — ContactModel3D.gains is a read-only property, verified, with no setter — so changing it is a 1.2 s OCP rebuild, and the panel offers a ladder of measured values rather than a continuous control that would rebuild on every drag. The registry is keyed on (kind, mode, Kp) for exactly this reason: all three change the built models and none can be changed on a built one.

5. What this cost, and what is still wrong

  • Peak torque went up on the default mode, 0.55 → 0.86 of limit. A restoring term is work. elbow+wrist is the way out if that becomes the binding constraint.
  • The forearm still carries almost nothing in the default mode. Kp = 50 did not fix that and was never going to — a contact constraint does not allocate force. The mode is chosen for the arm pose it produces, and the load path is elbow + wrist regardless of what the mode is called.
  • The palm site is still anchored in mid-air in every mode that names it. The certification warns about it now instead of passing silently, but the site itself should move onto a surface the gripper can actually reach, and every mode containing it is due a re-rank afterwards.
  • Nothing here has been on hardware. These are in-process MuJoCo holds. Kp = 50 tightens a constraint in the planner's model; how that lands through a real servo against a real table is a separate question, and the torque headroom is the number to watch.
  • The non-monotonic ladder is unexplained. The warm-start-mismatch story is a hypothesis. Re-solving each gain from scratch at that gain would test it; only Kp = 50 was re-solved that way here.
