Contact selection for the H12 braced lean

A static formulation that decides which surfaces the robot should brace on to reach a given target — solved by enumeration over candidate contact sets, not authored by hand.

2026-08-04 · branch asset-unification · worktree /home/humanoid/Programs/mjpc_icra2026 · code in studies/

What this is

The lean pipeline in mjpc/tasks/humanoid_bench/lean/ currently specifies bracing contacts by hand: each strategy phase carries a contacts array of (robot body, point) ↔ (table, point) pairs plus a scalar brace_force_target. This study asks whether that array can be computed from the reach target instead.

The pipeline is three stages, all offline:

  1. Pose. Gauss–Newton IK, one QP per step (quadprog): feet pinned, reach target and bracing sites as weighted objectives, non-penetration as hard inequalities taken from MuJoCo's own narrowphase with a 25 mm look-ahead margin, trust region inside the QP.
  2. Statics. At that pose, solve Sᵀτ + ΣJᵢᵀλᵢ = qfrc_bias — base rows as hard equality, joint torques bounded by the actuator limits, friction as a linearised pyramid at μ=0.6, 4 contact points per foot.
  3. Selection. Enumerate every subset of the candidate sites (2⁵ = 32) and keep the admissible one minimising normalised actuator effort Σ(τ/τmax)².
Why enumeration and not an MIQP. The reference method for this problem class (SCSP, IEEE T-RO 26-0767) needs a mixed-integer program because it samples ~70 candidate points on an arbitrary object surface. Our surface is a flat table and our candidate set is five named links, so all 32 subsets can be solved exhaustively. The combinatorial machinery that paper needs is simply not required here.

Proof: the contacts are actually made

Each clip runs the IK from the stand_up seed into the braced pose, so the contacts are seen being established rather than only held. Markers are ghosted until that link is genuinely in contact with the table, then become solid — so a solid marker is MuJoCo reporting a real contact, not an assertion.

Structure: near reaches take a light palm brace; everything further takes the elbow + forearm pair, usually supplemented by a trunk contact. That near→far split was predicted before any of this was computed, and it emerges from a min-effort objective with nothing about "near" or "far" encoded.

Are they controllable? Two measurements

Force authority and release (controllability.py) — LP bounds on each contact's normal force over the feasible set, and whether the same pose stays in equilibrium with the contact deleted. At (1.00, 0.95):

Read this the right way round. Naively the trunk looks ~10× better placed. But low sensitivity means low authority: the hip contact depends on effectively one joint, so it cannot be steered — the whole body is placed and the hip lands where it lands. The arm sites are less precisely determined but carry 2.5–3 joints of redundancy with which to servo the contact. The trunk contact is not a controllable contact; it is a consequence of the body pose.
Recommendation. Gate trunk sites out on a steerability criterion (≥2 effective joints). It costs no coverage — every target has an arm-only solution — and the price is ~55 % effort and ~27 % peak load, leaving the worst case at 0.39 of limit. Efficiency is the resource we have most of; controllability is the one we are short of.

Model findings worth acting on

  • Gripper jaw geometry decides the palm result. With the jaws modelled (±106 mm from the grasp axis) the palm is placeable at 17/45 targets and wins none. With any jaw-free proxy (sphere, mount mesh) it is placeable 45/45 and wins 6. Nothing else about the primitive shape matters. The jaws are real, so the pessimistic number is the honest one.
  • Arm torque limits in the lean model are stale. Model carries shoulder ±32, shoulder-yaw/elbow ±14.4, wrist ±9.5. CL_Assets — which _gen_h12_base_limits.py declares the source of truth, cross-checked against h12_safety_layer/core/joint_limits.py — has ±40 / ±18 / ±19. The wrist is out by 2×. Joint ranges import correctly; actuatorfrcrange does not.
  • right_shoulder_yaw range is L/R mirrored: model [-3.01, 2.66] vs CL [-2.66, 3.01].
  • The stand_up keyframe has the manipulation object embedded 42.5 mm into the tabletop. Harmless to the planner, confusing to any static analysis.
  • mjpc/tasks/humanoid_bench/avoid/avoid.cc:702 breaks a clean GCC build (-Werror=unused-result on an ignored system()).

Limitations — what this cannot tell you

  • It is static. Every result is about an equilibrium existing, never about reaching it. The lean's real failure mode is dynamic — the capture point leaving the support polygon during the transition — and this formulation is blind to it. Treat the output as a prior for the planner, which is also how the reference paper frames its own selection stage.
  • Selected ≠ required. At many targets the legs alone are admissible and the chosen brace wins only on effort.
  • Single deterministic solve per cell. No seeds, no replicates.
  • The z = 0.88 row was unsolvable in the jawed model because the grasp point cannot sit 15 mm above the table with the jaw axis vertical. That needs a wrist-roll orientation task — the concrete form of "rotate the gripper 90° to brace on its side" — which is not implemented, so read that row as not solved rather than infeasible.
