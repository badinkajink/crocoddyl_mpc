Allen parity, and what the real table broke

Merging icra2026 onto the CL_Assets import, and re-running the contact-selection study against the table the robot actually stands at. Four of the five things that broke were latent bugs, not consequences of the new geometry.

2026-08-04 · branch asset-unification · follows Contact selection for the H12 braced lean · code in lean_analysis/

Read this first if you read the previous page. Two of its headline claims were wrong and are corrected below (§6): the CL_Assets limit import does propagate actuatorfrcrange, and right_shoulder_yaw is not mirrored. Separately, every force and torque number on that page was computed with the contact point in the wrong place — up to 13 cm out — and with the wrong arm bracing. The method survives; the numbers do not.

1. Parity with icra2026

The merge was smaller than expected: three conflicts, all the same edit. Both branches touched the jab keyframes for unrelated reasons — icra2026 moved the object anchor z 0.75 → 1.035 for the taller table, asset-unification clamped the elbow −1.00 → −0.95 to the serviced limit — so the resolution takes the icra2026 line and re-applies the clamp on top. Lean_H12_Hands.xml was deleted upstream and modified here; the deletion wins, and only comments in stabilize.{cc,h} still name it.

What arrived from icra2026 that matters here is the table:

The robot's pelvis sits at x = 0.19, so the near edge moved from 108 mm in front of it to 310 mm. That alone does most of the work on the incidental-trunk-contact problem: the hip and torso can no longer fall onto the table by accident from a nominal stance.

Two things the merge broke, found by wiping the staged build tree

copy_directory never removes a file a previous build left behind, so an asset that stops being copied keeps working until someone clears it. Both of these were invisible until then.

  • All three Avoid_H12 variants were dead at load. The CL_Assets mesh swap compared the top level of the two mesh trees — "all 90 files, byte-identical" — and missed meshes/skin/, 8 STLs CL_Assets does not carry at all. h1_mujoco keeps owning the sensor skin, the same split already documented for inspire/ and ur5_magpie.
  • Allen's new brace poses were outside their own joint limits. forearm_brace_{lean,reach,release} hold left_wrist_pitch at −0.471239, which is 0.50° past CL's −0.4625. MuJoCo does not warn — it silently clamps at mj_resetDataKeyframe — so the pose the planner started from was quietly not the pose in the XML. Same class as the jab elbow clamp, and the same signature: a pose tuned at a limit that later moved.

lean_analysis/parity_audit.py now checks all of this: every H1-2 task model loads (15/15), no keyframe sits outside its range (0), and the effective actuator torque bound is reported per joint.

2. The torque basis is a real open question

MuJoCo enforces min(<position forcerange>, jnt_actfrcrange), so the number the planner sees is not necessarily the number CL_Assets supplies. Three authorities disagree, all real:

0.9 × TAU_ESTOP is the torque-budget clamp h12_control_node actually enforces on every commanded joint (deploy_common.h kClampRatio). A plan that needs more than that is not executable, whatever the motor could do — which is why the analysis now defaults to it (TAU_BASIS=clamp). Allen already applied exactly this reasoning to the ankle (48.6 = 0.9 × 54), but in h1_2_modified_magpie.xml.patch, so only the Magpie variant has it and every other H1-2 model still plans on 60 N·m there.

Needs confirming against the hardware. The torso estop is 40 N·m against a 200 N·m URDF limit — a ratio of 0.2, far tighter than any other joint (knee is 1.0, ankle pitch 0.9, shoulder 0.8). It is also the joint that carries a lean. If that number is real, trunk-supported leaning is far more torque-limited than any model in the repo believes. Nothing has been retuned on the strength of it.

3. What the real table broke in the contact study

Five things. Only the first is a consequence of the new geometry; the rest were bugs the old slab was forgiving enough to hide.

3.1 No lateral constraint on brace sites

The IK pinned a site's z and clamped its x into the table footprint, and never constrained y. The old slab was 2.12 m wide so y was never binding. Allen's is 0.595 m, and sites promptly parked at exactly the right height just off the side of it — reading as placed on a 1.4 mm z-residual while MuJoCo's narrowphase correctly reported no contact at all. That disagreement is what exposed it.

3.2 The wrong arm was bracing

reach_target is 0.9047 −0.2348 1.0982 — the object is on the robot's right, so the right hand reaches and the left arm braces. The study had it the other way round. Seeding from Allen's brace keyframe while bracing the right arm asks the solver to lift the already-braced left arm off the table and put the right one down across the body. Under that crossed configuration no elbow/forearm set placed at any target over x = 0.80…1.40 — which reads exactly like "the forearm brace is geometrically impossible on this table", and is not.

3.3 The wrong seed, and why a local IK could not recover

From stand_up the IK cannot get an elbow onto the new table at all. Seeding from Allen's poses fixes that — but only forearm_brace_reach works. All three of his brace keyframes have the brace down (elbow and forearm 16–29 mm above the surface), but in …_lean and …_release the reaching hand hangs 23–27 cm below the table surface, behind its near edge:

forearm_brace_lean     reach hand (0.422, -0.299, 0.714)   dz = -0.271   dist to target 0.620
forearm_brace_reach    reach hand (1.207, -0.229, 1.135)   dz = +0.150   dist to target 0.305
forearm_brace_release  reach hand (0.413, -0.299, 0.757)   dz = -0.228   dist to target 0.602

From below the surface, the straight-line path to a target on the tabletop runs through the slab. A local Gauss–Newton IK with hard non-penetration cannot route around that: it has to move away from the target — up, over the near edge — before it can move toward it. Measured: 250 iterations, zero QP fallbacks, reach residual pinned at its seed value of 0.52 m. A perfectly healthy solver, sitting still. That is indistinguishable from "the target is unreachable" unless you look at the fallback counter, which the code was computing and throwing away.

3.4 The QP was solving for contacts in the wrong place

This is the serious one. The QP applied a point force at the nominal site — but the site is on the link axis, and the link touches the table on its collision capsule's surface. Measured offsets at solved brace poses:

sitenominal → actual contactconsequence
palm32 mmmoment-arm error of this size in every equilibrium equation, every torque, every slip margin and every support region
forearm41–56 mm
elbow47 mm
hip104–129 mm

It also assumed every contact normal is world-vertical. On this table the brace lands on the near edge — contacts cluster at x = 0.500 and y = 0.297, both edges of the slab — where it is not. So the friction cone was being built around the wrong axis on precisely the contacts whose slip margin is the question. resolved_contacts() now takes the point and the frame from MuJoCo's narrowphase, and the force variables live in the contact frame. The feet keep their deliberate 4-corner sole model.

3.5 The torque basis

Now a named choice rather than "whatever the model carries" — see §2.

4. The math

4.1 Pose

Gauss–Newton IK, one QP per iteration over the 33 robot DOFs. For step dq:

min   ½ dq' G dq − a' dq
s.t.  n_c' J_c dq  ≥  d_c − δ      for every near-contact c   (non-penetration, HARD)
      −Δ ≤ dq_i ≤ Δ                                            (trust region, inside the QP)

G and a accumulate weighted least-squares terms: the reach target on the reaching wrist, each selected brace site onto the table plane and inside its footprint in x and y, both feet held at their seed pose (weight 300), and a posture pull back toward the seed. Non-penetration rows come from MuJoCo's own narrowphase with a 25 mm look-ahead margin, so the constraint is predictive rather than corrective.

4.2 Static equilibrium, and which contacts it is over

At the solved pose, with q̇ = q̈ = 0, split the floating-base rows from the actuated ones:

base    Σ J_i[:, :6]'  λ_i  =  g[:6]                    (6 rows, hard)
joints  τ = g[6:] − Σ J_i[:, 6:]' λ_i ,   |τ| ≤ τ_max   (27 rows)

with g = qfrc_bias at rest — pure gravity, not mj_inverse, whose qfrc_constraint term folds in whatever the pose happens to be penetrating (20 kN at the seed keyframe, against the 664 N actually being balanced).

Each λ_i is expressed in its own contact frame as (λ_n, λ_t1, λ_t2), with λ_n ≥ 0 (unilateral: push, never pull) and the Coulomb cone linearised as a 4-facet pyramid:

+λ_t − μ λ_n / √2  ≤ 0
−λ_t − μ λ_n / √2  ≤ 0        for each tangent, μ = 0.6

Two rows per tangent, not one. Negating the whole row instead pins |λ_t| to the cone boundary rather than bounding it, and every LP comes back infeasible — a bug that cost a session.

The objective is w_τ ‖τ / τ_max‖² + w_λ ‖λ‖². The first term is what we care about — normalised actuator effort, so a knee at 129/300 counts the same as a wrist at 4/9.5. The second is the min-norm regulariser that makes load sharing across redundant contacts well-posed.

4.3 Slip margin

Per contact, straight off the solved forces:

u = ‖λ_t‖ / (μ λ_n)     u = 0 pure normal push,  u = 1 on the cone, about to slide

This is the metric for "the bracing arm slides along the table". It costs nothing — the forces are already there — and it turns out to be the constraint that actually binds (§5).

4.4 Static-equilibrium region

The multi-contact generalisation of the support polygon. Newton–Euler about the world origin, for CoM c and robot mass M:

Σ R_i' λ_i              = (0, 0, Mg)
Σ p_i × (R_i' λ_i)      = (Mg c_y, −Mg c_x, 0)

The moment is independent of c_z, so this is genuinely a 2-D set and needs no height assumption. Feasibility at a candidate (c_x, c_y) is one LP; the boundary is ray-shot in 36 directions with 18 bisection steps each. Two versions are computed and the gap between them is informative:

  • contact-only — the 6 equilibrium rows plus friction cones. Exact. What the contact set can do, ignoring whether the robot can hold it.
  • actuated — the same plus |τ| ≤ τ_max. Approximate: g(q) is frozen at the solved pose while the CoM is swept, so it is a linearisation about that pose, not a re-solved posture. Reported separately for that reason.

The robustness margin is the distance from the pose's own CoM to that boundary, in metres — one scalar per pose, comparable across contact sets. Max resistible push is the same LP with an extra horizontal force applied at the CoM, bisected per direction.

The formulation is cross-checked against itself. The QP balances MuJoCo's floating-base generalised forces; the region LP balances a hand-built world-origin wrench. They are different parameterisations of the same equilibrium, so a force distribution satisfying one must satisfy the other. self_test() checks exactly that, and caught both a sign error in the gravity moment and mj_getTotalmass counting the table and the object (153.7 kg against the robot's 67.7). Either bug made every region come back empty — which looks identical to "this pose is unstable".

5. Results

5.1 The brace buys 20 cm of reach — and the limit is friction, not torque

Target swept outward along x at Allen's y = −0.2348, z = 1.0982, left arm bracing, TAU_BASIS=clamp. Furthest target that is both solved (MuJoCo confirms the brace contacts exist) and feasible (an admissible force distribution exists inside the torque budget):

contact setreach limitgainpeak τ/τmax at the limitworst brace slip u over the range
legs only1.10 m—0.84— (feet 0.85)
palm1.30 m+200 mm0.851.00
elbow + forearm1.30 m+200 mm0.650.94
elbow + forearm + palm1.30 m+200 mm0.680.85
elbow + forearm + hip1.30 m+200 mm0.940.81
hip1.35 m+250 mm0.610.65

Two things fall out of this that were not visible before.

Unbraced, the robot runs out of foot friction and torque together. At its limit legs-only sits at τ/τmax = 0.84 and foot slip 0.85 — both about to give. Push one step further and the equilibrium margin goes negative.

Braced, friction becomes the first thing to give — but only for the pure arm braces. The picture is not uniform and it is worth being precise about which sets it applies to:

  • elbow + forearm (0.65 torque, 0.94 slip) and elbow + forearm + palm (0.68, 0.85) are genuinely friction-limited: they run out of grip with a third of their torque budget unspent.
  • palm alone saturates both at once (0.85 torque, 0.997 slip) — it is at the cone exactly at its limit.
  • elbow + forearm + hip is torque-limited (0.94), not friction-limited (0.81).
  • hip saturates neither (0.61, 0.65); it stops at 1.35 m because the contact stops being placeable, i.e. a geometry limit rather than a mechanical one.

So the sharp version of the claim is narrower than "bracing is friction-limited": the two- and three-point arm braces reach the friction cone well before they reach the torque budget, and those are the sets the contact selection actually picks in the mid-range. That is the slip visible in the clips. Nothing in the effort objective — which is what the selection minimises — can see it.

Consequence for contact selection. Ranking sets by normalised actuator effort — the objective on the previous page — selects sets that are cheap to hold and says nothing about whether they are about to slide. At μ = 0.6 the arm-brace sets are one perturbation from slipping at the far end of the envelope, while still holding a third of their torque budget in reserve. Slip margin belongs in the selection criterion, not just in the report.

5.2 The support region, drawn

Static-equilibrium CoM region at Allen's reach target, top view, world x to the right (toward the table), y up. Open circle is the pose's own CoM; grey dots are the resolved contact points; the dashed outline in each panel is the legs-only region, for reference.

Legs-only gives a 69 mm margin and the actuated and contact-only regions coincide — actuation is not what limits it. Adding an arm brace grows it modestly (87–91 mm) and opens a gap between the two versions (2–3 cm), i.e. the arm can reach equilibria it cannot hold. Adding the hip is a different regime: the margin quadruples to 277 mm, the CoM moves 18 cm forward, and the actuated and contact-only regions nearly coincide again — the trunk contact costs almost no actuator authority because it is carried by the structure rather than by a joint.

5.3 Trunk contact: good support, bad contact

The hip is the best contact in the table on every static metric — furthest reach, largest margin, lowest slip, cheapest in torque. It should still not be commanded, for two reasons that are now both measured rather than asserted:

  • It is not steerable. The contact-point sensitivity (precision.py) gives the hip an effective joint count of 1.0 — the torso joint, and nothing else. Arm contacts carry 2.5–2.9 joints of redundancy with which to actively servo the contact. The trunk contact is not a controllable contact; it is a consequence of the body pose.
  • The site is a poor label for it. The nominal hip site is 104–129 mm from where MuJoCo puts the contact — by far the worst of any candidate. Even with the force now applied at the right point, "hip contact" names a region of the pelvis rather than a point you can aim.

On the new table it is also largely moot at nominal stance: with the near edge 310 mm in front of the pelvis, the hip and torso no longer fall onto the table incidentally. The gate proposed previously — keep trunk sites as candidates but require ≥ 2 effective joints of contact-point authority, which hip (1.0) and torso (1.0) fail and all three arm sites pass — still stands, and now has a second justification.

7. Clips

These animate the IK converging, not a physics rollout — the pose is a static solve, and there is no controller in the loop. Markers sit at the contact MuJoCo actually reports (not the nominal site, which is up to 13 cm away) and are ghosted until that contact exists. The seed already has the brace established, so the arm no longer appears to skate across the table while the reach closes; what remains is the reaching arm moving, which is the motion being solved for.

8. What is still open

  • The torso estop (§2). Blocking for any claim about trunk-supported leaning. Nothing retuned until it is confirmed.
  • μ = 0.6 is assumed, and §5.1 says it is the binding constraint. The whole braced reach envelope now rests on a friction coefficient nobody measured. This is the single cheapest experiment left: at μ = 0.4 the palm brace is already sliding at targets it currently "solves".
  • Slip margin is not yet in the selection objective, only in the report.
  • The release test is static. It shows an equilibrium exists without a contact, not that the transition into or out of it is stable. Closing that needs a dynamic check this formulation cannot provide.
  • MJPC handoff. Emitting the selected set as a per-phase contacts array, and whether MJPC can refine to a pre-selected pose or wants an offline warm start. First data point: testspeed on Lean H12 Magpie runs 375 planning steps in 5.83 s wall — 0.51× realtime with 15 threads.
  • The magpie patch applies with fuzz on the two hunks whose context lines are joint tags, because the CL import rewrites range/actuatorfrcrange in exactly those lines. It lands correctly today; it is one limit change away from not doing so.
