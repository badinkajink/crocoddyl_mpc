An 11-term lean, and a contact mode you can ask for

Session 13. lean.cc is 3 657 lines and 35 cost terms, and on this robot, this table and this target it braces 8 times in 10 while leaving the reaching hand 41 mm further from the target than standing still would. This is the replacement S12 §9 scoped: eleven terms, no keyframes, no phases, no force feedback, and the contact mode as an input rather than a property of a hand-authored pose. It reaches every target it is given, 26 rollouts without a fall — and the thing the brace actually buys turns out to be invisible to the reach metric, which is why this page ends with the support-region measurement rather than starting with it.

2026-08-06 · branch asset-unification · task Lean Simple H12 Magpie · new mjpc/tasks/humanoid_bench/lean/lean_simple.{h,cc} + Lean_Simple_H12_Magpie.xml · runs and figures under lean_analysis/runs/2026-08-06_simple/

What this page claims. (1) An 11-term static cost reaches where the 35-term keyframe stack did not: +0.31 m to +0.70 m of reach gain against −0.041 m, same robot, same slab, same planner budget. (2) The contact mode is steerable — asking for the palm gets the palm, asking for the elbow gets the elbow — but not fully: the forearm parks 3–5 mm short unless the seat target is pushed 5 mm deeper, and "forearm alone" is never achieved. (3) The brace buys no reach at all, and buys +190 mm of CoM margin and a third of the torque budget. An unbraced rollout that looks identical on the reach metric finishes with its centre of mass 42 mm outside its own static-equilibrium region.

1. What this replaces

The braced lean has been carried by lean.cc: 3 657 lines, 35 active cost terms, eight hand-authored keyframes per strategy, and a 14-second open-loop ramp that interpolates toward them. S12 measured what that buys. It braces 8 times in 10. Its reaching hand finishes 41 mm further from the target than standing still would leave it. The term named after the objective sits 15th of 35 by weight, and the thing that actually sequences the motion is the keyframe ramp, with MJPC acting as a 1-second-horizon stabiliser tracking it.

§9 of that page proposed a replacement rather than a repair: per-link signed-distance contact costs, no force feedback, task-space invariants instead of pose targets, and an objective that dominates its own phase. Lean Simple H12 Magpie is that proposal, built on the shape of lean_simple_gripper.cc — the 385-line variant that was stashed rather than developed — and sharing only the model with lean.cc: same robot, same slab, same reach target, same CEM settings. Nothing else is shared, so a difference in outcome is a difference in the cost.

Distance, not force

Contact force is an output of the constraint solver: discontinuous across make and break, and differenced by a 10-sample CEM it is mostly noise. Signed distance is defined while the link is still in the air, has an unambiguous gradient the whole way in, and saturates once the link is down — an attractor with a floor, so it stops fighting the contact solver instead of pulling forever, which is what Brace Pos did by aiming 115 mm inside the wood. Brace Force has no counterpart here.

The mode is one input, in one place

The keepout term reads the seat weights rather than keeping its own list of links. A mode expressed in two places is a mode that can disagree with itself: asking for {elbow, forearm} automatically means "and keep the palm off", and asking for {palm} automatically means "and keep the elbow and forearm off", with no second edit.

3. Three things that were measuring the wrong quantity

All three were found while building the task, and each had already produced, or was about to produce, a number worth publishing and then retracting.

3.1 mj_geomDistance is wrong on this robot's arm

The 2026-08-05 per-site experiment in lean.cc introduced Brace Elbow/Forearm/Palm Seat, each computed as mj_geomDistance() minimised over the link's collidable geoms — chosen explicitly because it "handles the mesh links correctly and needs no per-link calibration". It does not. *_shoulder_yaw_link and *_elbow_link carry no primitive collision geom at all; their only collidable geom is the visual mesh. Lifting the whole robot vertically from the shipped braced keyframe:

The capsule column tracks the lift 1:1. The mesh columns are a third of a metre out at the moment of contact and are not even affine in the lift: 400 mm of lift moves them 503 and 603 mm. MuJoCo's own narrowphase reports the arm meshes in contact at lift 0, so −333 mm is not a real penetration — the function is simply not usable on these geoms. Two of the three per-site seat terms in that experiment were therefore reading noise, which is enough on its own to explain why it produced nothing.

Lean Simple measures the world z of the lowest point of an explicit shape instead: the body's collidable primitives where it has any (the gripper box and its two jaws, the torso box, the hip capsule, the pelvis sphere), and a declared capsule where it has none (the upper arm and the forearm). Exact for the shapes given, orientation-correct against a horizontal plane, one calibration, done once, in §4.

3.2 Height is not clearance, and clearance is not distance

Two versions of the keepout were wrong in opposite directions. The first charged a link for being below the slab's top plane — but at the home key the robot's torso box bottoms out 57 mm below the table top while standing 240 mm clear of the near edge, so the term reported the trunk as buried in a table it was nowhere near, and was saturated before the robot moved. Adding the horizontal miss fixed that, and introduced the second error: a top-face measure cannot see an edge contact. The first far-target rollout duly rested its torso box on the slab's near edge — MuJoCo reporting contact at exactly 0.0 mm, at x = 0.500, which is the edge — while the top-face measure read +50.4 mm of clearance. The keepout now uses mj_geomDistance against the slab for the trunk and the gripper, which are all primitives and where the function is exact.

3.3 A rollout that does not carry its own target cannot be scored

The far-target cells override a model numeric on the command line (--numeric "reach_target=1.15|-0.2348|1.0982"). The scorer read the target from the XML. So the first far-target run was scored against a target 245 mm from the one it was given, and reported +0.068 m of reach gain for a rollout whose hand finished 4 mm from its actual target. This is S12 §2's error with the sign flipped — there a missing baseline turned no reach into a large one; here a wrong target turned a completed reach into a failed one. testspeed now writes the --weights and --numeric strings it was given into the CSV header, and every consumer reads the target from there. A trajectory that does not record what it was asked to do is not evidence.

4. What "seated" means, calibrated once

A seat cost needs a distance that is zero where MuJoCo starts reporting contact — not before (the planner stops short of the table) and not after (it demands penetration it cannot get, which is the Brace Pos defect). lean_analysis/seat_calib.py fits that by translating a braced pose vertically in 5 mm steps and recording both the analytic measure and whether the narrowphase reports link-vs-slab contact:

linkmeasure at the last frame WITH contactat the first frame WITHOUTso contact begins at
elbow (upper arm)+1.2 mm+6.2 mm0…6 mm
forearm−0.3 mm+4.7 mm0…5 mm
palm (gripper)−0.9 mm+4.1 mm0…4 mm

One constant serves all three: the seat term saturates at 0, which keeps a little tension on past first contact and never asks for penetration. Over the same sweep the measure tracks the lift 1:1 for every link, which §3.1's table shows the alternative does not.

The gripper needs its jaws. A keepout written against *_gripper_collision alone reported 40 mm of clearance while left_gripper_jaw_a was already on the wood for 638 of 1000 sampled frames — the jaws hang 80 mm below the palm box and touch first. Measuring "every collidable primitive on the body" rather than one named geom fixes it without a special case.

5.1 The reach is solved, at every target

+0.309 m at NEAR, +0.553 m at FAR, +0.692 m at VFAR, no falls in 26 rollouts, and the settled hand is within 1–14 mm of the target in every cell. Against S12's −0.041 m on the same robot and table this is not a marginal improvement, and it costs 11 terms and no keyframes. It is also, on its own, uninformative about the brace: every cell reaches, including the ones told to touch nothing.

5.2 The mode is steerable, and not fully achieved

Contact duty per link over the settled window; solid = down more than half the time.

  • Palm: asked for, achieved. far_palm has palm duty 0.99 in both seeds and nothing else on the slab.
  • Elbow: asked for, achieved. far_brace and near_brace both seat the upper arm at duty 1.00, resting 0.9–1.5 mm off the measured surface.
  • Forearm: 3–5 mm short. Its seat gap settles at 2.9–5.4 mm — outside contact, inside the noise. At weight 300 a 3 mm gap is 0.9 cost units against a 10-sample CEM, which is nothing. Pushing the saturation point 5 mm INTO the slab — inside §4's own calibration uncertainty, and physically stopped by the contact solver anyway — lifts forearm duty from 0.00 to 0.56 (far_seatdepth). So this is a resolution limit, not a modelling error.
  • "Forearm alone" is never achieved (far_forearm: none / palm). Asking a serial chain to put its middle link on a plane while holding the link beyond it off is a geometrically tight request, and one seed satisfied it by putting the palm down instead — a mode violation the keepout did not prevent.
  • Asking for all three gets palm, and elbow half the time; the forearm is squeezed out between them.
  • More samples help the marginal case. At 40 trajectories instead of 10, far_brace reaches forearm duty 0.50 rather than 0.00 — consistent with the resolution story, not with a cost error.

One braced rollout at FAR. The three seat gaps and (dashed) the reach error. The reach is finished by t ≈ 4 s; the elbow comes down later and the forearm never quite does. Ordering matters — see §6.2.

6. What the brace actually buys

Reach cannot see it, so this is the measurement that matters. These are the same static-equilibrium CoM regions as the S11 handoff page §5 — the multi-contact support region of Bretl & Lall (2008) / Caron et al. (2015), ray-shot at 10° and bisected to ~6 µm, solved both contact-only and with the actuator torque envelope — with one change: they are computed at the pose the rollout actually reached, with the contact set MuJoCo's narrowphase actually reports, rather than at a pose the offline IK designed. 6.1 The unbraced rollouts are not standing, they are hanging on their actuators

Every rollout that reaches FAR with nothing on the table finishes with its centre of mass outside its own static-equilibrium region — −42 and −23 mm, with the full torque envelope already granted. There is no admissible set of contact forces that holds those poses. MuJoCo runs them anyway, because the position servos are saturating: peak |τ|/τmax reaches 1.00 in the rollout telemetry, for 15% of frames in far_brace_s0. On hardware that is an e-stop, not a reach.

Every rollout that seats its elbow finishes at +131…+150 mm of margin using 0.55–0.76 of the torque budget. The legs-only column says why: at those same poses, legs alone give −46…−70 mm. The pose is one the robot cannot hold, and the brace is what makes it holdable. That is the entire case for bracing, it is worth about +190 mm of margin and a third of the actuator budget — and it shows up as zero on the reach metric.

The palm-braced cells make the same point from the other side. far_palm is inside its region by 5 mm and vfar_none by 4–31 mm, but their min-effort force distributions need 2.0×, 2.8× and 5.7× the torque limit: a gripper 100 mm from the wrist is a long moment arm on a 19 N·m joint. Contact-only margins of +86…+182 mm alongside actuated margins near zero is exactly the signature of a contact the arm cannot afford to use, and it is why the actuated region is worth computing separately.

Read the τ column as the min-effort distribution's worst joint, not as a feasibility verdict: it minimises ‖τ/τmax‖₂, not the maximum, so it can exceed 1 at a pose whose actuated margin is still positive. The feasibility verdict is the sign of the actuated margin.

The braced rollout reaches first and braces second: it is 88 mm outside its region at t = 3 s, holding the extended pose on saturated actuators, and the elbow does not arrive until t ≈ 6 s. Everything §5 reports about the reach is achieved during a window in which the robot is not in static equilibrium. A cost that made the seat term dominate until the contact is established, and only then released the reach, would produce the same endpoint through a feasible path; a single static weight vector cannot express that. This is the clearest argument found so far for phases — real ones, gated on a measured contact rather than on a 14 s clock.

The unbraced rollout is not innocent either. From t = 3 to t = 9 s it is braced on its hip — resting the trunk on the table edge, which Trunk Clear exists to prevent — and its margin is +257 mm while it does. It then comes off the table and spends the rest of the rollout statically infeasible. So "no mode requested" did not mean "no contact"; it meant an unrequested, unmodelled, transient trunk contact, which the settled-window duty column reports as 0.00.

7. The support region has to know about the brace

The balance term measures the capture point against a support region, and the first version used the feet alone. That is the humanoid_bench region, and it forbids exactly the excursion a brace makes safe: measured at FAR under a feet-only region, the robot seated its elbow and then refused to extend. The region now extends forward to a brace link whose measured seat gap is inside 10 mm — asking for a contact widens nothing until the link is actually down.

The A/B is honest but weak: far_brace_nosupport reaches +0.551 m against far_brace's +0.549 m and lands at +165 mm of margin against +136 mm. At these targets the extension is not what makes the reach happen. It matters in the transient — it is what stops the balance term from paying the robot to abandon a brace it has already established — and the case for it here is mechanical rather than empirical. brace_support=0 restores the feet-only region from the command line so the comparison stays available.

8. Watching it

9. What is still wrong

9.1 The tremor is unchanged, because it is the algorithm

Command churn is 27–61×, the same band S12 measured on the old task. It should be: nothing here touches the planner. Re-estimating a distribution over 81 parameters (27 actuators × 3 spline knots) from 10 draws while keeping 6 as elite produces an update dominated by sampling noise. A cost rewrite cannot fix that and this one does not claim to.

9.2 Reach-then-brace is the wrong order, and the cost cannot say so

§6.2. A static weight vector has no way to express "establish the contact, then extend". This is the one place where the old task's phases were solving a real problem — and the fix is not the 14 s open-loop ramp but a gate on the measured seat gap, which this task already computes.

9.3 Mode exclusivity leaks

Three cells put a link on the slab that was not requested (near_none s1 and far_forearm s1 got the palm; far_brace_horizon s1 got the trunk at 0.47 duty). The keepout is a soft 200-weight term against a 400-weight objective: it is a preference, not a constraint, and the numbers say so.

9.4 The forearm needs a steeper norm, not a bigger weight

The last 3 mm are worth 0.9 cost units. Raising the weight scales the whole basin; what is wanted is curvature near zero — a smaller smooth-abs parameter on the seat terms, or a rectified quadratic in the final centimetre. The seat_depth knob is the blunt version and it works (§5.2), but it works by moving the target rather than by sharpening the cost.

11. Caveats

  • Two seeds, unseeded. The planner RNG is not seeded on this branch, so the repeats are independent draws, not a controlled comparison, and two of them bound very little. The margin result is the one that survives the small n: it has the same sign and the same order of magnitude in every braced and every unbraced rollout measured.
  • The margins are static. They ask whether a pose is holdable at rest, not whether the trajectory that reaches it is dynamically sound. A negative margin under a saturated position servo is a strong warning, not a proof of failure in simulation.
  • Simulation only, μ = 0.6 at the slab, one robot placement, one table height. S11 §2 showed the friction assumption is not de-risked at the envelope edge.
  • The support-region extension is geometric. It asks whether a seated link is forward of the toes, not whether its friction cone can carry the implied load. §6.1 shows why that gap matters: the palm modes are geometrically fine and actuator-infeasible.
  • No completion condition. The rollouts run for a fixed wall clock. A deployment needs "the hand is on the target and the brace is loaded" as an exit, not a timer.
  • The reach point is the *_hand site (wrist + 170 mm) where S11/S12 used wrist + 130 mm. Reach gain is a difference and is insensitive to the 40 mm; absolute residuals here are not directly comparable to those pages.
