Foot motion and command feasibility during a lateral wrist pulse

16 October 2026 · Foot-motion checkpoint · Two 6.02 s MuJoCo rollouts · Repository baseline 7c0240e

A 90 N lateral wrist pulse increased left-sole displacement from 0.14 to 78.46 mm after disturbance onset, with 210 ms of lost loaded floor contact. Final reach error increased from 17.6 to 271.1 mm. The predictor retained all 35 nodes but requested negative contact forces and infeasible wrenches. Reactive reanchoring changed neither the next state nor contact force because this plan has zero foot-position stabilization gain. The actuator audit found a second failure: position-servo input clipping changes requested torque by up to 44.67 N m in the disturbed run.

Terms and metrics

CMPC is this repository’s Crocoddyl model predictive controller. Displacement and rotation are the largest sole-centre translation and rotation-angle change from the named interval’s first sample. Tangential displacement uses world x/y. Contact loss means no floor witness on that ankle body above 1 N, sampled every 2 ms. Loaded slip is the largest horizontal velocity of a foot material point at a floor contact above 5 N. It distinguishes sliding from sole-centre motion caused by rotation.

Prediction residual is the root-mean-square (RMS) distance between the next estimated sole pose and the solver’s first future state, 20 ms later. It includes shooting defects and actuator realization. Fixed-anchor residual is distance to the original plan’s sole centre. Normal force is signed compression; negative values request tension. Tangential force is the horizontal force magnitude. Cone violation is distance outside each linear row bound: rows 0–4 use N, foot rows 5–16 use N m. These rows are soft penalties.

Centre-of-pressure (CoP) margin is the smallest remaining distance to an assumed support boundary; negative is outside. Wrench CoP uses signed force and moment at the sole centre and requires normal force above 5 N. Mesh pressure margin uses the normal-load-weighted contact position projected into the sole’s bottom polygon. Speed is the largest joint absolute velocity or point translational speed. Acceleration is an adjacent 2 ms velocity difference. Startup covers 0–0.5 s; hold covers 4–6.02 s; post-pulse covers 4.5–6.02 s. Position tracking is RMS joint command minus simulated position.

Plan, observation and experiment

The archived h0885_x1060/cell/plan_elbow_forearm.json uses a 0.885 m tabletop, a 2.4 s approach and 80 braced nodes. Its target is (1.060, −0.2348, 0.9982) m and its additional velocity cost is zero. The reconstructed staged Magpie model has 200 action nodes. Index 165 selects the final 35-node, 0.70 s window; all 302 solves in each episode retained 35 nodes at 0.02 s. The saved plan reports converged=false; its is_feasible=true field does not certify contact inequalities.

Both runs use MuJoCoPlant.read → default_sense(seed=17) → State → policy. This existing estimated-state error path adds constant base-position and orientation biases drawn with 10 mm and 0.01 rad standard deviations. Joint and velocity channels are ideal. Those S17 assumptions have no new hardware calibration. Scoring and rendering use ground truth. The opt-in runner uses one solver iteration, one thread and zero transport compensation for synchronous simulation. No controller, plan, site membership or default was edited.

The measured premise was the archived plan’s 12.11/7.54 mm foot-reference errors and the height audit’s startup motion. The hypothesis predicted greater sole motion and residual under one 90 N world +y force on the reaching wrist from 4.50 to 4.80 s. Amplitude and duration come from the earlier 30/60/90 N, 0.3 s study; direction and onset are scenario assumptions. The conditions are identical before 4.5 s to machine precision. Both displacement and estimated residual increased, satisfying the pre-recorded comparison.

Both clips contain 181 frames at 30 fps and last 6.033 s. Simulation timestamps select the nearest recorded pose with at most 0.667 ms rounding. The final recorded state is at 6.018 s; the last video sample is 6.000 s. Frame sequences at startup, approach and pulse onset/release were inspected.

MuJoCo contact force uses the normal and two tangent axes in the contact frame; it acts on geom 2 and is sign-reversed for geom 1. Forces are summed on each ankle body, moments shifted to the sole centre, then rotated into the moving sole frame for the CoP calculation. Per-contact normals remained nonnegative. Native pyramidal-cone excess was below 0.000001 N for the recorded contacts, while the robot still slipped. Reconstructed foot wrenches agree with live logs within 0.000069 N and 0.000069 N m.

The collision sole has a 250 mm long, at most 76 mm wide bottom polygon at body z = −45 mm. The predictor uses a 200 × 80 mm rectangle centred at x = 33 mm, z = −55 mm. MuJoCo floor friction is 1.0; the predictor coefficient is 0.6. Thus a negative margin against the predictor rectangle alone does not establish an impossible simulated pressure distribution. Nominal pressure reaches the mesh edge; the disturbed left pressure projection extends 2.50 mm beyond the bottom polygon as contact moves onto other foot surfaces. The wrist-roll body and other unplanned bodies also contact the table; their forces are retained in the raw records.

Joint-position commands are clamped before conversion to MuJoCo’s position-servo input. Feedforward and desired-velocity terms then shift that input outside its own bounds. Clipping changes the realized torque by 11.16 N m at left hip yaw in the nominal run and 44.67 N m at right hip yaw in the disturbed run. Reconstructing the servo with both input and force clipping matches logged actuator force within 0.1 piconewton metre. Actuator force-bound violations are zero. This comparison measures executable simulation commands; hardware torque and velocity tracking remain unmeasured.

Runtime, artifacts and limitations

Solver p95 time was 22.92 ms nominal and 22.54 ms disturbed on one CPU. 62/302 and 50/302 solves exceeded the 20 ms period. Simulation ran synchronously with no wall-clock delay injection. Rollout loops took 7.47 and 7.30 s; all guarded numerical jobs, including failed probes, rescoring and video, used 53.58 s. Two of six permitted rollouts were used, with no rollout retries. Resguard recorded no new event (197 entries before and after). Peak RSS was 3,038,720 KiB; subsequent full runs require at least a 3.8 GB cap. The first asset-path probe and first scoring attempt failed before completion and are retained.

This two-run comparison measures one synthetic state-error realization and one pulse. Hardware friction, estimator slip response and actuator bandwidth remain unmeasured. The archived schedule has no recovery phase. Force scoring samples the full horizon at 10 Hz and can miss intermediate peaks.

Future work

The next contact-model comparison should first correct the opt-in sole geometry/friction representation and reject or fall back from tensile forces and violated wrench rows. It should reuse this nominal/pulse pair and require feasible node-zero commands plus lower estimated one-step residual. A subsequent sliding model must predict tangential motion and contact loss; reference updates alone cannot do this.

The motion-transfer checkpoint should compare the original startup against one command-realization or initialization correction before re-solving a longer or velocity-regularized approach. Measure first-0.5 s joint acceleration, wrist speed at first brace load, combined-input clipping and realized torque. Improvement requires smaller peaks and clipping error while retaining reach and support. Hardware validation needs synchronized command, encoder, IMU/base estimate and current/torque logs, plus independently measured sole motion and surface friction. Recovery still needs a separate release/abort experiment.
