MJPC braces, and does not reach

Session 12, second pass. The first draft of this page reported that MJPC enters a brace 8 of 10 times, holds it, reaches while braced, and returns — and concluded the handoff could stay in MJPC. The bracing is real. The reaching is not: across eight independent braces the reaching hand ends where it already was standing still, and the contact forces I used to argue the rest were computed with the actuators switched off.

2026-08-05 · branch asset-unification · task Lean H12 Magpie, strategy 22 · runs under lean_analysis/runs/2026-08-05_session12/, re-scored in place

What this revision retracts from its own first draft.
• All contact-force numbers. Computed with d.ctrl at zero against <position> actuators — the forces of a robot commanded to the all-zeros pose. §1.
• “Phase 0 survives by cheating — 492–758 N through the trunk, at or above body weight.” True load is 45 N in one seed and 398 N in the other. §6.
• “Arm load ramps 65 → 89 → 85 → 57 → 57 → 24 → 23 N” as evidence of progressive unloading. True: 113 → 153 → 138 → 113 → 130 → 108 → 84 N. It does not unload. §6.
• “Phase 1 holds a genuine brace … MJPC sits in it for 12 s.” That seed has an arm on the table 10% of the time. §6.
• “MJPC gives up 0.34–0.54 m of reach and takes margin for it — a different objective optimised competently.” There is no trade: the reach gain is −0.041 m. §2.
• “The handoff can stay in MJPC … there is no longer a capability gap.” §5, §7, §10.

What survives unchanged: the 9999 phase-gate mechanism (§8), the 200 N·m torso basis and the finding that it binds nothing, and the structural contact agreement — which is computed from qpos alone and is untouched by the force bug.

1. The scoring bug

Every per-frame number came from cheap_frame(), which re-solved a logged pose:

d.qpos[:] = q
d.qvel[:] = 0
d.qacc[:] = 0        # no-op: mj_forward overwrites it
mujoco.mj_forward(m, d)

d.ctrl is never assigned, so it stays at its initialised zero. Every actuator on this robot is a position servo — <position forcerange="-200 200" kp="150" kv="5"/> and similar, kp 40–200 across the tree. ctrl = 0 is not “no command”; it commands every joint to angle 0. So each frame was re-solved as a robot being driven hard toward a pose it was not in, and the contact forces reported are that robot's, not the rollout's.

The fix is to replay the logged command with the logged velocity:

d.qpos[:] = q;  d.qvel[:] = qv;  d.ctrl[:] = u
mujoco.mj_forward(m, d)
Blast radius. Contact detection depends on qpos alone, so which links touch the table was always right — §5's structural result stands. Reach residual, margin, tilt, pelvis height and peak torque are also qpos- or log-derived and did not move. Only the forces were wrong, and they were the evidence for three of the first draft's four conclusions.

The trajectories are fine; only the scoring was broken, so this is a re-score rather than a re-run — mjpc_chain.py --rescore reads the CSVs already on disk. The numbers below therefore still describe the rollouts in the videos.

2. The reach never happens

The first draft reported reach as a residual — 0.348–0.551 m — and compared it to the QP's 0.007 m. It never compared it to the only baseline that makes the number mean anything: where the hand already was before the robot did anything. At t = 0, standing, the right wrist point is 0.352 m from the target.

Eight braces, zero reach. The best run gains 4 mm; the mean is negative. A 27-second stand → lean → reach sequence leaves the hand slightly further from the target than standing there would have. The QP pose, for comparison, is on the target.

So “MJPC trades reach for margin” was not a finding, it was a missing baseline. There is no trade — one side of it is zero. And the extra margin the first draft credited MJPC with (+0.118…+0.174 against the QP's +0.094) is the margin of a robot that has leaned on a table without extending, which is not the same quantity the QP is maximising subject to.

One run is worth singling out. enter seed 4 ends brace_lean with the hand 0.127 m from the target — the closest approach anywhere in the study. Then brace_reach switches on, Reaching Hand Dist goes to weight 80, and the hand ends the phase at 0.351 m. Turning the reach cost on moved the hand away from the target.

3. Which arm goes to the target

Watching the chain video rather than its summary row: the limb that visibly travels toward the target is the bracing arm. Measured, on the same rollout —

reaching hand (right wrist + 0.13 m) bracing forearm site (left elbow link) reaching hand at t = 0, standing

The bracing forearm closes from 0.747 m to 0.378 m — it travels 0.37 m toward the target and stays there. The reaching hand oscillates about its starting value for 66 s and ends 0.30 m further out than it began. Through brace_reach the mean distances are: reaching hand 0.496 m, bracing forearm 0.412 m, bracing elbow 0.386 m — both bracing links are closer to the reach target than the reaching hand is.

And lean.cc is measuring the wrong arm too. The metrics block at lean.cc:3354 reads
// Right arm always braces, left arm always reaches (lean.cc convention).
double *reaching_hand = left_hand;
while the model sets <numeric name="reach_hand" data="2"/>, which Residual() resolves to right hand reaches, left arm braces. So reach_from_pelvis and reach_beyond_foot_edge — the metrics the deploy monitor surfaces — are computed on the bracing arm for this strategy. Not a control bug; the residuals are unaffected. It does mean the deploy-side reach telemetry has never measured the reaching hand.

5. A contact set is not a contact mode

The structural result stands: in eight independent braces MJPC touched the table only with {elbow, forearm}, never the palm, wrist or trunk — the same links the enumeration selects, and the ones it rejects stayed rejected. That is computed from qpos and the force bug does not touch it.

But the first draft used it to argue that MJPC reaches the selected mode, and a mode is a pose, not a list of link names. Joint-space distance from the QP solution to MJPC's pose at the end of brace_reach, over the 27 actuated joints:

That is the reaching arm's shoulder, and §9 of the first draft identified it as the joint that actually carries a braced lean (ratio 0.46–0.47, the binding joint at every contact set tested). The single joint the certificate most depends on is the single joint MJPC most disagrees about, and the disagreement is in the direction of not extending.

Nothing in these runs shows the selected mode is dynamically reachable. Two links touching the right slab is a much weaker statement than “the planner found the certified pose”, and the first draft spent that weaker statement as if it were the stronger one.

That part of the first draft was measuring something real. It just was not measuring a reach.

7. What MJPC is actually configured as here

The first draft asked whether the planner could find the brace. The more useful question is what the planner is being asked to do at all. From the model:

SettingValueConsequence
agent_planner5 = Cross Entropyderivative-free, resampled every step
sampling_trajectories10ten rollouts per planning iteration
n_elite6the update averages the best 6 of 10 — a 60% elite fraction, so the “selection” barely discriminates
sampling_spline_points327 actuators × 3 knots = an 81-dimensional decision variable
agent_horizon1.0 sthe manoeuvre it is “planning” is a 14 s ramp
This is a 10-sample stochastic search over 81 dimensions, keeping 6 of 10 samples as elite, looking 1 second ahead at a 14-second manoeuvre. Two things follow directly, and both are visible in the data above.

The chatter is the algorithm. Re-estimating a distribution over 81 parameters from 10 draws, and averaging 60% of them, produces an update dominated by the sampling noise rather than by the cost. That is the 14–55× churn in §4, and it is why even plain standing never settles.

MJPC is not planning the brace — the keyframe ramp is. With a 1 s horizon the planner cannot see past the current phase's setpoint. The thing that actually sequences the lean is target_ramp_sec = 14 interpolating toward a hand-authored keyframe, with CEM acting as a short-horizon stabiliser around a setpoint that is being dragged. “MJPC does the task” was never the right description of this: a human authored the trajectory as keyframes, and MJPC tracked it, badly.

That also reframes the 2/10 entry failure. It is not a planner failure and not a cost failure — it is an open-loop ramp toward a pose, with a 1 s-horizon stabiliser that cannot anticipate the topple and no abort condition (both the abort and the auto-advance are disabled by the 9999 pair, §8).

8. What survives from the first draft

The 9999 gate. Strategy 22's eight keyframes each carry "success_sustain_time": 9999.0 and "time_limit": 9999.0, and the XML default residual_Phase = -1 selects auto-advance, whose only two exits are those two timers. So a headless run spends its entire duration in keyframe 0, whose weight map has Reaching Hand Dist, Brace Pos, Brace Force and Contact all at 0.0. Session 11's “MJPC rejects the brace” was measured under the stand cost with every brace term switched off. That finding is correct and is the reason this session exists.

The --phase_schedule harness. lean.cc is still untouched at zero lines; the sequencing lives in testspeed.cc and the CSV carries a phase column so a rollout cannot be scored without knowing what it was being asked to do.

Torso torque. 200 N·m adopted from default_safety_full's estop (torque_ratio 1.0 × the URDF's 200), and it changes nothing: the torso carries 1.0–1.9 N·m, about 1% of its ceiling, and every derived number agrees to four decimals across a five-fold change in the limit. The joints that carry a braced lean are the reaching arm's shoulder pitch (ratio 0.46–0.47) and the hips (0.29–0.32). The S11 warning about e-stopping the torso was aimed at an idle joint.

What the enumeration proves. Unchanged, and §5 leans on it harder now: the choice among the 24 contact modes is exhaustive and the feasibility of each is a certified LP, but the pose within a mode is a single damped-least-squares descent from one seed, the contact locations are five hand-placed sites nobody is optimising, and dynamic reachability is out of scope — which §5 now shows is not a hypothetical gap.

9. Scoping the replacement task

The current cost stack does encode real engineering and should not be thrown away. But it is not steerable, and the weights say why. At brace_reach:

The objective — put the hand on the target — sits 15th of the 35 active terms: thirteen outweigh it outright, and the only thing it ties with is a term nobody enabled on purpose (§9.1). It is outweighed roughly 9:1 by the brace-position term alone and by an order of magnitude by the whole pose-imposing stack. That is exactly the non-interpretability: you cannot predict where the hand goes from where you put the target, because the target barely participates.

9.1 Three costs are on during the brace only because the JSON forgot them

Building that table turned up something that is not a weighting opinion but a bug. TransitionLocked fills a phase's weight vector from the JSON map and falls back to the XML default for any name the map omits:

auto it = kf.weight.find(name);
next_phase_weights_[i] = (it != kf.weight.end()) ? it->second : xml_default_weights_[i];

Three terms with non-zero XML defaults are explicitly zeroed in five of the eight keyframes and omitted from the other three — which are, precisely, the three brace phases:

Term (XML default)stand_upbrace_lean brace_reachbrace_release3 × standback, stand_up
Right Foot Lift (180)01801801800
Support Polygon (80)08080800
Body Yaw (60)060604040
During the braced lean the robot is being asked to lift its right foot, at weight 180. Every other keyframe in the strategy sets "Right Foot Lift": 0.0 deliberately; the three brace phases just do not mention it, so it comes back at its XML default. It is opposed by Foot Right Up at 2000, which is presumably why the robot mostly stays planted — but this is a 180-weight term pulling against a 2000-weight term for the entire 14 s entry ramp, in the exact window where 2 of 10 rollouts topple. Zeroing these three in the brace keyframes is a three-line JSON change and should be the first thing tested, before any redesign. It is also the cleanest available example of why the current task is not interpretable: nothing in the strategy file says these terms are on.

9.1 The shape I would build

  • Per-site contact costs, one per candidate link, on signed distance. Replace the single Brace Pos (which hardcodes one pad against one table point, matching one hand-authored keyframe) with an elbow, a forearm and a palm term, each a one-sided penalty on the site's signed distance to the table plane, saturating just inside the surface so it behaves like an attractor that stops pulling once seated. Then the offline enumeration's output — a subset — maps onto the task by switching terms on, and “which contacts do you want” becomes an input rather than a property of a keyframe.
  • Agreed on dropping force feedback. Brace Force should go. Contact force is an output of the constraint solver, discontinuous across make/break, and it is being differenced by a 10-sample CEM — the estimator sees mostly noise. Signed distance is continuous, defined when the link is in the air, and has an unambiguous gradient. If a load target is genuinely needed, it belongs as a post-hoc check or a slow outer loop, not inside the sampled cost.
  • Retire the pose-imposing terms, but not into thin air. Symmetry, Base Height, Lateral Center, Posture, Left Leg Anchor and Brace Arm Plane are doing the work of keeping the robot standing, and deleting them outright will produce a squat or a collapse. Replace them with the task-space invariants they are proxies for: CoM inside the support polygon, foot contact maintained, pelvis height as a lower bound rather than a target, joint and torque limits as barriers. Fewer terms, each one a thing you would defend to a reviewer. Note that Support Polygon already exists in lean.cc and is — by the omission in 9.1 rather than by anyone's decision — the only balance-region term currently live during the brace, at weight 80. Turning it into the deliberate balance term is a smaller step than it looks.
  • Make the objective dominant in its own phase, or make it a constraint. If the reach term is not the largest term in brace_reach, the phase is not a reach phase.
  • Score reach gain, not reach residual, in every future sweep — the whole error in §2 was one missing baseline.

Two things this will not fix on its own, and it is worth saying before the sweep rather than after. The 1 s horizon still cannot see a multi-second weight-transfer, and 10 samples over 81 dimensions is a weak search however the costs are shaped. Reweighting is cheap and worth doing; expecting it to produce a precise, legible reach is optimistic. The planner budget (sampling_trajectories, n_elite, horizon, knot count) should be swept in the same experiment as the weights, or the result will be attributed to the wrong knob.

10. The solver question, revisited

The first draft asserted that “a DDP with a prescribed contact schedule would have exactly the same failure, faster”, and treated DDP taking the mode schedule as an input as a mark against it. That was wrong, and the objection is a good one.

Crocoddyl is built for precisely this problem: whole-body multi-contact trajectory optimisation over a prescribed contact sequence, with the contact constraints entering the dynamics rather than the cost, friction cones and torque limits as constraints, and impulse models for the make/break transitions. Needing to be told the contact schedule is not a limitation here — the schedule is exactly what the offline enumeration already produces, and it is the one thing in this pipeline that is not in doubt (exhaustive over 24 modes, feasibility certified by LP). The two halves fit together; I argued they did not.

The sharper way to see it is §7. What the current system calls planning is a human-authored keyframe ramp; MJPC is a 1 s-horizon stabiliser tracking it. The open question was never “sampling MPC or DDP” — it is “who authors the trajectory”, and the honest answer today is: a person did, by hand, in keyframes, and the result is 1.5 rad from the certified pose with zero net reach. Replacing that author with a trajectory optimiser that respects the dynamics and the contact schedule is the obvious move, and it is what crocoddyl is for.

The architecture that follows.
1. Enumeration → contact mode + certified pose q* (have it).
2. Crocoddyl → dynamically feasible stand → q* → stand trajectory over that contact schedule, with the friction cone and torque limits as constraints (missing today; the 14 s hand ramp is the placeholder).
3. MJPC / a WBC → track it, and reject disturbances. This is what MJPC is actually good at, and the part it is currently being asked to do that it does adequately.

Under this split the task rewrite in §9 is still worth doing — step 3 needs a cost that expresses “track this trajectory, keep these contacts, stay balanced”, which is a much smaller and more defensible stack than the current 30 terms.

Risks worth costing before committing, so the estimate is not just the happy path: crocoddyl is a local optimiser and needs a warm start (the existing keyframe ramp is a serviceable one); its rigid contact model is not MuJoCo's soft constraint model, so any trajectory it produces must be validated in MuJoCo before it means anything; and it brings a Pinocchio model of the robot that has to be kept in sync with the MJCF, which is a real maintenance surface. None of these is a reason not to try it. All of them are reasons to try it on the brace_lean entry alone — the 14 s window that produces 2/10 of the failures and 100% of the reach shortfall — before porting the whole mission.

12. Caveats and reproduction

  • Ten samples, unseeded. The planner RNG is not seeded on this branch, so no rollout is reproducible run-to-run. 8/10 is roughly 44–97% at 95% confidence — it supports “usually enters, fails identifiably”, not a reliability figure. The CSVs are kept and the re-score is deterministic given them.
  • The reach result is not sample-limited. Eight braces, gain range −0.199 to +0.004 m. The spread is small and the sign is consistent; this one does not need more seeds.
  • Dwell times are mine. The phase schedule is an experimental choice, generous at 16 s for a 14 s ramp. A deployment needs a completion condition, not a clock.
  • One target — reach_target = (0.9047, −0.2348, 1.0982) — and simulation only, at μ = 0.6, which S11 §2 showed is not de-risked at the envelope edge.
  • Re-score, not re-run: python3 mjpc_chain.py --rescore --episodes … --outdir runs/2026-08-05_session12/<dir>. The trajectories are the ones in the videos.
